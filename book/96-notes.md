# Notes

Every *Research says* and *Myth check* in this book rests on a published study. These are the titles, with the journal and the year, chapter by chapter, so you can look one up and ask it the second question. Each callout says how far its study goes. Three studies are left off the list by the author's choice: the one on how well people understand devices (*Start here*, *Know what you think*, *The explanation*) and the two behind the *Myth check* in *Voice, body and camera*.

- *Start here*: "Speakers' overestimation of their effectiveness," *Psychological Science*, 2002.
- *The exact word*: "Consequences of erudite vernacular utilized irrespective of necessity: Problems with using long words needlessly," *Applied Cognitive Psychology*, 2006.
- *How sure are you? Say that*: "Do people inherently dislike uncertain advice?" *Psychological Science*, 2018; "Exploring the 'planning fallacy': Why people underestimate their task completion times," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1994.
- *With a machine in the room* (Part I): "Automation bias and errors: Are crews better than individuals?" *International Journal of Aviation Psychology*, 2000; "Complacency and bias in human use of automation: An attentional integration," *Human Factors*, 2010.
- *Thinking on your feet*: "Slow lies: Response delays promote perceptions of insincerity," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2021.
- *Listening people can feel*: "Changes in medical errors after implementation of a handoff program," *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2014.
- *Questions and common ground*: "Do conversations end when people want them to?" *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 2021.
- *The meeting*: "The impact of leader eye gaze on disparity in member influence: Implications for process and performance in diverse groups," *Academy of Management Journal*, 2021.
- *The update*: "Do you want the good news or the bad news first? The nature and consequences of news order preferences," *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 2014.
- *The demo*: "Service recovery paradox: A meta-analysis," *Journal of Service Research*, 2007; "A longitudinal study of complaining customers' evaluations of multiple service failures and recovery efforts," *Journal of Marketing*, 2002.
- *The pitch*: "When good evidence goes bad: The weak evidence effect in judgment and decision-making," *Cognition*, 2011.
- *The sale*: "If you need help, just ask: Underestimating compliance with direct requests for help," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2008.
- *The brief*: "The expertise reversal effect," *Educational Psychologist*, 2003; "Accountability and automation bias," *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies*, 2000.
- *On the page*: "Egocentrism over e-mail: Can we communicate as well as we think?" *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2005.
- *Across borders*: "The extreme illusion of understanding," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 2022.
- *The hard conversation*: "Do you want the good news or the bad news first? The nature and consequences of news order preferences," *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 2014.
- *Arguing well*: "When good evidence goes bad: The weak evidence effect in judgment and decision-making," *Cognition*, 2011.
- *Negotiation*: "Disconnecting outcomes and evaluations: The role of negotiator focus," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2002; "First offers as anchors: The role of perspective-taking and negotiator focus," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2001.
- *Pressure and how to resist it*: "Dispelling the illusion of invulnerability: The motivations and mechanisms of resistance to persuasion," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2002; "The truth about the truth: A meta-analytic review of the truth effect," *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 2010.
- *Engineers and architects*: "Automation bias and errors: Are crews better than individuals?" *International Journal of Aviation Psychology*, 2000; "Complacency and bias in human use of automation: An attentional integration," *Human Factors*, 2010.
- *Data scientists*: "Algorithm aversion: People erroneously avoid algorithms after seeing them err," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 2015; "Automation bias and errors: Are crews better than individuals?" *International Journal of Aviation Psychology*, 2000; "Complacency and bias in human use of automation: An attentional integration," *Human Factors*, 2010.
- *Founders and the AI-era startup*: "Automation bias and errors: Are crews better than individuals?" *International Journal of Aviation Psychology*, 2000; "Complacency and bias in human use of automation: An attentional integration," *Human Factors*, 2010.

Most of these studies were read by their abstracts, and the callouts claim no more than those state. None was commissioned for this book, and any of them may turn out to be wrong. If one does, the advice stands or falls with the reasoning around it.
