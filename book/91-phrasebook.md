# The phrasebook

Lines to borrow when the moment is close and your own words are not. Each was written for the chapter named under its group, which also says when the line backfires. Read them aloud before you need them, and replace anything in brackets.

## Find your point and say it first

- To open with the point: "In one sentence: [the sentence]. The rest is why."
- To name a gap before someone else does: "I can't tell you yet what this rests on. I'll know by Thursday, and I'll tell you either way."
- To invite the refusal: "Tell me if you would say no to this, and why."
- "I need one thing from you today: [the request], by [when]."
- "I'll start with the end." (when you hear yourself starting from Monday)
- "Is that the part you need, or would the reason help more?"

From *Know what you think*, *Give it a shape*.

## Know who is listening

- "So I can bring the right [numbers / detail]: where does the answer go after [the meeting]?"
- "Who will you need to explain this to afterward?"
- "The number you can take to [their meeting] is [number]. Here is what it rests on."

From *Who is listening*.

## Say how sure you are

- **The basis.** "I checked this myself." "I was told this by [who or what], and I haven't checked it." "I worked it out from [what]." "I'm assuming [what]. Nobody has checked it."
- **A number.** "Between [low] and [high]. It's [high] if [one thing] happens."
- **"I don't know."** "I don't know yet. What I do know is [what]. I'll know by [when]."
- **When they need one answer.** "If you need one date for your plan, use [the later one]. I'll tell you by [when] if [the earlier one] holds."
- "Nobody knows yet. Here's what we're doing meanwhile, and why."

From *How sure are you? Say that*, *Leading when nobody knows the answer*.

## Make the word exact, and the sound easy

- "Ready for us to test, or ready for your team to use?"
- "By [word], I mean [what someone can see or do]. Is that what you mean too?"
- "I don't have the exact English word. Here is what happened."
- **A number someone will act on.** "Fifteen minutes. That's one-five." "It's in the chat now."
- **A time, across time zones.** "[time], [city] time."
- **A "not" that decides something.** "We cannot test on Thursday. The earliest is Friday."
- **When you didn't hear.** "Sorry, fifteen or fifty?" "Was that 'can' or 'cannot'?" "I missed the end of that sentence. Could you say it again?"

From *The exact word*, *Easy to hear*.

## Listen so they can feel it

- **The three parts.** "What I understood is [their point, in their words]." "What I'll do is [one action] by [day]." "What I'm not sure of is [one thing]."
- **To make correcting you easy.** "What did I miss?" "Which part did I get wrong?"
- **When they have to repeat something.** "You've said that twice, so I haven't shown you I got it. Here's what I have: [your read-back]."
- **The follow-up.** "[Their word]. What happens then?" "Who gets the call when that happens?"
- **To go first.** "We had the same problem. [One true thing that happened.]"
- **When a face changes at your ask.** "Before I go on: does [the ask] cause a problem on your side?" "What would you need to know before you decide?"
- **To turn a nod into a decision.** "Can I write that down as a yes to [the ask]?"

From *Listening people can feel*, *Questions and common ground*, *Voice, body and camera*.

## Meet, explain, update

- **To sort an item.** "Are we deciding this, or hearing about it?"
- **When you are the most senior.** "I haven't promised this yet. What should I know before I do?" "I'll go last."
- **To leave it open.** "We can't decide this today. [Name] will bring [the missing fact] by [day]."
- **Before you prepare.** "When someone asks why you approved this, what do you have to be able to answer?"
- **To open.** "There are two ways to do this. Here is what each one does to [what they answer for]."
- **When agreement comes too fast.** "Before you agree, what would you still need to see?"
- **When a date moves.** "You'll hear this from others today. I wanted you to have it from me." "[New date], if [one thing]. I'll know by [day]."
- **When there is no other date.** "If that fails, I don't have a date yet. I'll have one by [day]."
- **To give the count.** "That was close to an ordinary [day]: [the number, in their units]." "This is one case. I haven't counted the others yet."
- **When someone tells you a story.** "Was that a typical [day]?" "What are you asking me to decide?"

From *The meeting*, *The explanation*, *The update*, *Story for people who think in systems*.

## Present, demo, recover

- **To promise the ask early.** "By the end I'll ask you to decide one thing: [the decision]."
- **When someone asks early.** "I'll answer that now." Then: "Back to [the question we were on]."
- **When your time is cut.** "I'll skip to the decision and the one risk in it."
- "The screen has stopped. Here is what I know."
- "Your live system is not affected. [Name] has just checked."

From *The presentation*, *The demo*.

## Think on your feet

- **To sort before you answer.** "Let me think."
- **A challenge.** "That's fair: [the true part]. From [when], [commitment]." "That's not quite what happened. Here's what did, and how we know: [what]."
- **A judgment.** "My view is [view], because [the one thing I weighed]. The decision is [name]'s."
- **A commitment you can't make.** "I can't promise that. I can promise you an answer by [day]."
- **An interruption.** "One sentence to finish this, then yours."

From *Thinking on your feet*.

## Pitch, sell, brief, write

- **To find their reason.** "What does a bad week look like where you are now?" "What else are you choosing between?"
- **To find who decides.** "Who else has to agree, and what will they ask you?"
- **An admission, upward.** "We haven't shown [what] yet. We'll know by [when]."
- **When they ask "Can it...?"** "It could. We haven't built it. What would you use it for?"
- **A lower quote.** "I haven't seen theirs run. What test would you trust for both of us?"
- **A no.** "That's clear, thank you. What would you need to see?"
- **To hand it over.** "I need [what], because [the one reason]." "It's done when [what anyone could see]." "How you do it is yours."
- **To mark what you know.** "I checked this. I'm only assuming that."
- **When it comes back.** "What did you have to assume?" "Which part are you least sure of?"
- **To open.** "Please [do this] by [day]: [the reason]." "Nothing needed from you. [What changed], and [what it means for you]."
- **When nobody answers.** "Did this reach you? If I don't hear by [day], I'll [a step we can undo]."

From *The pitch*, *The sale*, *The brief*, *On the page*.

## Work across borders

- **When the room changes language.** "Take the time you need. Could I have the result in English when you're there?"
- **When you can't tell what the answer was.** "Should I plan on that, or not yet?"
- **To make a no easy to give.** "If it's a no, please tell me by Friday. I can plan around a no."
- **When nobody answers.** "I haven't heard from you, so this is still open. From [day], waiting costs us [what]."

From *Across borders*.

## Say the hard thing

- **To open.** "This is about [subject]. [What isn't at stake.]"
- **To leave room.** "The record shows [what]. I don't know what came before."
- **Your part.** "My part was [what]. By [day] I'll have changed it."
- **When it's aimed at you.** "Which day should I look at?" "You're right about [part]. I need until [day] for the rest."
- **The no.** "No, not by [day]. [The one reason.]" "Ask me again on [day], when I'll have [what]."
- **The trade.** "[This] or [that] by then. [The check] stays in both."
- **To someone senior.** "Is this decided, or still open?" "Could I tell you why I see it differently, before you decide?"
- **The graceful exit.** "Thank you for asking me first. I'd like to know what you decide."
- **To ask.** "Next time, could you [one act] before [one moment]?"
- **To close.** "So from [day], I'll [act] and you'll [act]. What would you change?"

From *The hard conversation*, *Saying no and pushing back*, *Difficult people*.

## Argue, trade, hold

- **Their side.** "Let me say your side, and you correct it."
- **Before a test.** "Before we look: which result would change your mind, and which would change mine?"
- **A changed mind.** "On [day] I said [claim]. Since then [what happened]. I now think [new claim]."
- **An ending.** "We disagree about one thing: [it]. Until [name] decides on [day], we'll [what happens meanwhile]."
- **To ask about a number.** "What does that figure rest on?" "When does the saving have to show?"
- **To trade.** "If you can [what I need], I can [what it costs me]."
- **To close.** "In [month], which of these will be hardest for you to keep?"
- **To leave.** "I don't see terms we'd both keep. If [one thing] changes, please call me."
- **When they push back.** "I understand it's more than you planned. The rate stays the same, and the start can be smaller."
- **To ask for the reason.** "I see the date. I don't see the reason yet."
- **To sign less.** "I can put my name to [the smaller claim] now."
- **To push honestly.** "I'm pressing because I need this by [day]. You should know that."

From *Arguing well*, *Negotiation*, *Say less*, *Pressure and how to resist it*.

## Answer for work a machine drafted

- "I read [what]. I ran [what]. I didn't check [what]."
- "An agent drafted it, I read it, and my name is on it."
- "What happens here that nobody would think to write down?"
- "No, I didn't calculate it. I redid [piece], and it matched."
- "If [this] happens, stop using it and call me."
- "It missed. Here's what I know, and when I'll know more."
- "Building it took [time]. Reading it takes [time], and we have [time]."
- "That's on the board. What don't you trust yet?"
- "Somebody briefed it, read it and chose its tests. What did each of us know?"
- "I promised [three], and I have [two]. Until that line is filled, [what happens]."
- "A machine made [what]. [Name] checked it against [what]. It could still miss [what]."
- "You decide [what], without asking me first. [The other decision] stays with [name]."
- "What would stop you from deciding this?"

From *Engineers and architects*, *Data scientists*, *Product managers and agile leads*, *Founders and the AI-era startup*, *Leading when nobody knows the answer*.
