# Templates and checklists

Each form comes from the chapter named beside it, which shows it at work and says when it fails.

## The ten tools

### The point sentence

`[this listener] should [believe or do this], because [the one reason]`

- Could the listener say no to it?
- Would they know what to do next?

Before any line that someone else will read (*Know what you think*).

### The second-question test

1. What is the point?
2. What does it rest on?
3. Where does it stop?
4. What do you want from me?
5. Who answers for it?

The first four before you speak or send; all five when a claim reaches you (*Start here*; *How sure are you? Say that*).

### The listener sketch

`[name] knows [what last reached them]; has to decide [what, after your meeting]; stands to lose [what goes wrong for them if your answer turns out wrong]`

When a message asks someone to decide, or carries bad news; then check it with one question (*Who is listening*).

### Next-question order

1. Start with the point sentence.
2. After each sentence, ask what this listener would ask next, and answer that.
3. Stop when the next question is theirs to ask.

For any answer, explanation or update (*Give it a shape*).

### The observable version

- For any label, say what a recording would show or a number would state.
- Could two people who disagree about the label agree that this happened?

For words that cross a boundary or carry a decision (*The exact word*).

### The sized claim

- **The claim.** One sentence: what is true, or what will happen.
- **The basis.** How you know it, in one of four words, strongest first:
  - **Checked.** I ran it or read the record myself.
  - **Told.** A person, a document or a machine told me.
  - **Reasoned.** I worked it out from things checked or told.
  - **Assumed.** I am taking it as given.
- **The limit.** Where the claim stops, what has not been checked, and what would change it.

For a date: `[date], if [the one thing it depends on]. If not, [the other date]. I will know by [when].`

For claims someone will act on (*How sure are you? Say that*).

### Sort first

| Kind of question | What settles it |
|---|---|
| Fact | The fact and how you know it; if you don't have it, when you will |
| Judgment | Your view, what you weighed, and whose decision it is |
| Commitment | Yes or no, with its condition, from someone who can keep it |
| Challenge | The true part, said first; then sort what is left |

| A disagreement about | What to agree |
|---|---|
| What is true | The test, before anyone argues the answer |
| What to do | The goal |
| Who gets something limited | A fair rule, and the name of who decides |

Before answering a hard question or joining a disagreement (*Thinking on your feet*; *Arguing well*).

### The read-back

- What I understood.
- What I will do.
- What I am unsure of.

When you receive, and asked for when you give; not when the stakes are low and the other person wants speed (*Listening people can feel*).

### The working story

- **The decision**, written first, never told first.
- **One moment**: a time, a place, a person, one thing said or seen.
- **The count**: the number that shows the moment stands for a pattern, or the honest statement that it is one case.
- **The handover**: the decision you ask for, in one sentence.

Under ninety seconds, a limit this book sets; never in place of the answer to a direct question (*Story for people who think in systems*).

### The preparation sheet

- what I do with no deal;
- where I would walk, and why;
- what I aim for, and why it is defensible;
- my guess at their three;
- the issues and what each is worth to each side;
- who decides on their side;
- what I will not do;
- and the test: will both sides still keep this once the work has started?

One page, before any negotiation (*Negotiation*).

## Forms for the rooms

### The first minute of a hard conversation

- "This is about [subject]. [What isn't at stake.]"
- Your facts, your part, your limit and your question. Then stop.

For feedback: `[what a record would show] [what it cost] [what I may have wrong] [what I'd like next time]`

For the talk you're putting off; leave out any promise nobody can make (*The hard conversation*).

### The apology

- What did you do?
- What did it cost them?
- What don't you know yet?
- What has already changed?
- Where can they see it?
- Have you crossed out every "if", every "but" and every request?

For one you owe (*The hard conversation*).

### The brief

`I need [what], because [the one reason]. Done when [what anyone could see], and [what must stay as it is]. Checked: [facts]. Assumed: [facts]. Send me your plan before you start. [One name] reads it before [the next step].`

For work you hand to a person or an agent (*The brief*).

### The meeting close

- Before the discussion: "Who decides this one?"
- Before that person speaks, to whoever is closest to the work: "What have you seen that we should know first?"
- Before the next item, to the owner: "What will you do, by when, and what are you unsure of?"

For one item, whether or not you run the meeting (*The meeting*).

### The sign-off

Before you run it: `If [input], then [what happens].`

- What you read.
- What you predicted.
- What happened.
- What you didn't check.

Before you sign a change you didn't write (*Engineers and architects*).

### A no for the decision log

`No to [what], because [what it could reach]. The cost is [what we give up]. [One fact] would change my answer. [Names] can refuse this.`

When others should be able to check and refuse your no (*Engineers and architects*).

### Who answers the phone

`From [when] to [when], [name] answers, by [how]. Nobody holds [which line] yet. Until somebody does, [what happens].`

For a customer's worst hour (*Founders and the AI-era startup*).

### One page for a question with no answer

- **The question**, in their words, and its kinds
- **The direction.** `We are [doing this], because [the one reason].`
- **The unknown.** `Nobody knows [what]. We know [what], [basis word]. It stops at [limit].`
- **Who decides.** `[name] decides [what], without asking.`
- **The next test.** `On [day], [what we do]. [Name] counts [what].`
- **What would change my mind.** `If [result], then [the other path].`
- **If it fails anyway**, what we do, and who hears first
- **What I won't do**
- **Can everyone named still keep this once it has started?**

When people keep asking what nobody can answer (*Leading when nobody knows the answer*).

### The standards page

- Before an agent starts, one engineer's name goes on the work. That engineer reads every change the agent writes, small ones included, runs it against tests a person wrote, and signs it.
- Under the signature: what was read, what was run, what wasn't covered.
- The agent's first instruction: "Put the reader's name in your first line. If you have no name, stop and ask."
- At `[customer]`, the signature reaches `[reviewers]` `[how long]` ahead, and one objection stops the change.
- A change that needs new access waits `[how long]`.
- When more is waiting than one day's reading, the agents stop.
- Every `[day]`, `[name]` gets the list of what went live, with the name of who read it.
- This rests on `[the test, and what it found]`. We run it again in `[month]`.
- It holds until `[when]`. `[One event]`, or `[another]`, reopens it the next morning.
- `[Name]` answers for this page.

Before a customer asks who checks what your agents write (*Who does well now*).
