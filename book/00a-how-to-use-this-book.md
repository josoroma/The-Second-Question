# How to use this book

This book is for people who build things and then have to get other people to understand, agree and act. Founders and solo operators come first. Beside them are engineers and architects, data scientists, product managers, agile leads, and the offshore and forward-deployed engineers who work across a border.

It trains you to find your point, say it at the size the facts allow and stay composed when the second question comes. It trains you to put the same questions to any claim that reaches you, a colleague's or a machine's, before you act on it.

## Three ways in

- **The night before something.** Open *Find your moment*, the situation finder, and do the one practice in the row closest to yours.
- **A chapter a week.** Read in order, and take each chapter's marked drill into one real conversation.
- **A plan.** *The training plan*, at the back, runs 30 days at ten to fifteen minutes a day, or 12 weeks. Both are targets this book sets.

Whichever way you choose, do one thing first: the recording in *Start here*. Make it before you read past that chapter's first page, because you can't be unprepared twice. Every later recording, at the end of each part, is compared with it.

## What the labels mean

- **Line to keep** is the chapter's claim in one sentence. Read alone, the chapter titles and these lines give the whole argument.
- **Research says** sets a published finding beside the technique it supports: what was observed, what it means for you, where it stops.
- **Myth check** corrects a famous claim that is wrong.
- **From practice** is craft knowledge, labeled so you don't take it for a finding.
- **Practice** teaches one technique. Most show a first draft a capable person would really say, what's wrong with it, a better version and your turn. **Done means** names an outcome a stranger could check.
- **If you do only one, do this one** marks one drill in a chapter. The training plan is built from these.
- **Words to use** gives phrases for the moment. Learn what a line does, then say it your own way.
- **When it backfires** says where the advice fails, and what changes when the other person has power over you.
- **Spar with an AI** is a prompt to paste into any assistant, to rehearse against somebody who pushes back.

## The critical-thinking strand

Six chapters train you to test a claim before you act on it: *Know what you think*, *How sure are you? Say that*, *Thinking on your feet*, *Arguing well*, *Pressure and how to resist it* and *Seeing the system*. The second of them shows how to test plausible output from a machine.

## If you work in a second language

An accent and a second language are normal working conditions here, and you are never asked to lose either one. English is a second language for most of the people in the scenes, the most senior among them. Decide your point in whichever language is faster, then carry it into English in short sentences. *Easy to hear* trains you to be understood with the accent you have, and *Phrases you will hear* gives idioms their plain meaning. Most chapters close with a line written for you.

## Practice safely

*Safe practice: record only yourself, or others only with their consent and within your employer's rules.*

*Safe practice: before you paste anything into an assistant, change names and figures and leave out anything confidential, or use a tool your employer has approved; treat its feedback as one agreeable opinion, not a grade.*

One of these lines returns wherever a drill records or uses an assistant. Every timed drill has an untimed or written form. A pause that comes from a stutter, a second language or thinking time is never scored.

Nothing here is legal, medical or financial advice. Some behavior isn't a wording problem at all, and the last section of *Difficult people* says where to take it.

## How this book was made

The people and companies are invented, and each scene is built to show a technique. One small cast lives through nine months of work, from March to November.

The book was written in November 2026. AI agents drafted it, directed by the author and working from the author's brief. Each sentence that says or implies research shows something is tied to a row in a ledger of published studies. Nearly all of those studies were checked through their abstracts, and the notes at the back list most of their titles. Every other number is a figure inside an invented example or a target this book sets.

No study has tested the book as a whole, so let your own recordings do it. However it was drafted, the author answers for it.
