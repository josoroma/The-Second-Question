# The training plan

Thirty days, ten to fifteen minutes a day. The plan is built from the drills marked **If you do only one, do this one**, in the book's order. Read each chapter when you like; reading time is extra. Every count of minutes, takes, days, weeks or months on these pages is a target this book sets.

Start on the first day of your working week, so that the drills that need a colleague fall on working days. On a day off, a friend can be the listener. Stop at fifteen minutes, even unfinished. When a drill ends in a real conversation, the fifteen minutes cover your preparation; the conversation is part of your work. When *Done means* waits for a reply, go on to the next day.

The eight recordings fall on Days 1, 2, 8, 13, 17, 22, 26 and 30. The middle five are the ones the book sets at the end of Parts I to V; move them if you must, but keep each after its part. Record sixty seconds on the same prompt: what you are working on, and why it matters. Run the four checks (sentences before the point, hedges with no basis, one example, the ending), then *said back*. Counting fillers is optional, and pauses, restarts and searching for a word never count. Written form: three minutes without stopping.

*Safe practice: record only yourself, or others only with their consent and within your employer's rules.*

Days 3, 7, 11, 24, 25 and 27 train the critical-thinking strand: *Know what you think*, *How sure are you? Say that*, *Thinking on your feet*, *Arguing well*, *Pressure and how to resist it* and *Seeing the system*.

## When you miss a day

Do the next drill on the next day you have, and move every later day back by one. Never put two days into one. If you miss a recording, make it before any new drill, because a take from later in the plan can't stand in for it. If you stop for a week, start again from your last recording. When a colleague isn't free, use the drill's written or untimed form if it has one, or repeat an earlier drill.

## A lighter month, and a second language

**A busy month.** Do only Days 1, 2, 3, 7, 9, 15, 18, 24 and 30, two or three a week.
Leave the rest without catching up; they can wait for a quieter month.

**In a second language.** Find the sentence in whichever language is faster, then say it in English; take the written or untimed form whenever there is one, and skip the filler count.
On Days 12 and 20, repeat *your own sheet* instead, starting with the numbers line, and judge it only on what your listener wrote down.

## How to tell it's working

Compare yourself only with your own earlier recordings. Other people's scores, and the model answers in the book, aren't your measure. A count that moves between two takes may be a change in you or a different week, so wait for a third take before you believe it.

The recording shows how you do on one prompt. Whether it reaches your real rooms shows in what people do next: the decision they make, the date they repeat, the question they no longer have to ask. Once a month, write a page of three lines:

1. **What changed.** One check that moved, and between which two dates.
2. **What it rests on.** One real conversation, with its day, and what the other person did.
3. **Where it stops.** The room you haven't tried it in, and the drill you'll take there next month.

That page is a sized claim about you. It makes no promise, and neither does this plan: it gives you the practice, the recordings and a way to see for yourself. If three months of pages show nothing, change the drill, or ask a listener what they hear that you don't.
