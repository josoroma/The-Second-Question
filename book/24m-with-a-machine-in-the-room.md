# With a machine in the room

*In a meeting: ask the question it leaves out*

"Site one trial, Friday 18 September. Fourteen items, fourteen green. What we count: doors the supervisors change. For this call: are we ready?"

An assistant Tolva approved for client work drafted those lines for Thursday's call, from the tickets and the plan. All Friday, Tolva's scheduler would plan the doors at Veldhaven's first warehouse, beside the old paper plan, and the supervisors could overrule it. Go-live was the 28th. Tyler, Tolva's delivery lead, checked each green against its ticket, and all fourteen were right.

But everyone could say yes to "are we ready?" and still decide nothing. The page said what to count. It did not say how many would stop the go-live. Tyler wrote a new question: "How many changed doors would be too many, and who says so?" The page had a line for everyone on the call except one: site one's morning supervisor, who would be changing the doors.

On the call, Tyler opened with his new question.

"As few as possible," said site one's manager.

"And you?" Tyler asked the supervisor.

"I'll change four whatever it does. A forklift is out, or my crew is at the other end."

Claire, who runs operations at Veldhaven, looked up. "And a truck that can't unload at the door it was given?"

"That one should stop you."

"Then we count those. Six or fewer, we go. Seven, we stay on paper. I'll say which by eight tomorrow evening."

Tyler said it back: "Claire decides by eight. Trucks that can't unload: up to six, we go. Seven, paper."

"And if nobody can reach me," Claire said, "site one's manager decides, on the same number."

That evening the assistant turned Tyler's notes into a summary: "The team confirmed readiness and agreed clear criteria for the trial." The number and the name were gone. He put them back, word for word. On Friday the supervisors changed eleven doors, and five counted. At ten to eight Claire wrote: "Live on the 28th."

## What can't be prepared

A decision is somebody's word, given to the people who will act on it. Let an assistant prepare everything around it: the status, the agenda, the hard questions, the note. Check its facts, and enjoy the time it saves.

> **Line to keep:** Handed a perfect page, a room can still decide nothing.

Three jobs stay with you in every room:

- **Choose the question.** Write one that someone could answer no to, and beside it the name of whoever settles it.
- **Read what nobody asked.** Find the person with no line on the page and ask what they will do. An assistant can list possible gaps; the forklift was in the supervisor's head.
- **Say the decision back,** owner first, while the owner can still correct you. Then make the note say the same.

## Practice: a question that can get a no

**With a person.** **If you do only one, do this one.**

**Situation.** Tomorrow you lead a call about moving a client's backups to new storage. The old copies go on the 1st.

**First draft (what an assistant writes).** "Today we'll review the backup migration, confirm that all workstreams are on schedule and surface any remaining risks. Any concerns?"

**What's wrong.** It asks everyone, so nobody is asked, and silence will pass for a yes. The colleague who restores files isn't in it.

**Better.**

"One decision today: do the old copies go on the 1st? You do the restores. Have you restored anything from the new storage?"

"Not yet."

"What would you need to see first?"

"One full restore. I can try it by the 24th."

"So it's yours: one full restore by the 24th, or the old copies stay. Did I get that right?"

**Why it works.** The question can get a no and goes to whoever has the missing fact. The decision has an owner and a day.

**Your turn (10 minutes, then a real meeting this week).** Have an assistant turn your notes into one page. Replace its question with yours, and ask the person named beside it first. At the end, say the decision back, aloud or in writing. A reply of "we" or "soon" has no owner yet. **Done means** the owner confirmed or corrected your sentence, and someone who wasn't there can tell who decides what, and by when.

*Safe practice: before you paste anything into an assistant, change names and figures and leave out anything confidential, or use a tool your employer has approved; treat its feedback as one agreeable opinion, not a grade.*

## When it backfires

**Your question leaves others out.** Claire's number told her about a day. On site one's first night live the scheduler stalled for forty minutes, and Thursday's call hadn't asked about a night. Ask each decision the second-question test's *Where does it stop?*

**The room belongs to someone senior.** Send your question ahead: "Could we also settle [this]? I think it's yours to answer."

**Nothing needs deciding.** A question forced into a briefing sounds like an audit.

**In a second language.** The moment may pass while you find the words. Ask late: "Before we finish, can I go back to one thing?"

## Record again

Sixty seconds: what you are working on, and why it matters. Write the point you meant, then check four things: sentences before the point, hedges with no basis, one example, an ask or next step at the end. For *said back*, someone from one of your rooms hears it once and writes the point in one sentence.

Counting fillers is optional; pauses, restarts and searching for a word never count. **Written form.** The same prompt, three minutes of writing.

Then compare the endings of all your takes: this part practiced asking for a decision.

*Safe practice: record only yourself, or others only with their consent and within your employer's rules.*
