# Leading when nobody knows the answer

People who hear "nobody knows" still have to decide what to do tomorrow. On Thursday 19 November Lucía, Tolva's chief executive, left that part out, in front of her whole company.

Tolva's software already chose the door for every truck at two warehouses of Veldhaven, the Rotterdam food wholesaler. The third, the cold store, would go live in four days, with the December peak right behind it. On the company call, Tolva's support engineer unmuted. She shares the nights with Mateo, Lucía's co-founder.

"Does it hold in December? All three sites at once?"

Lucía gave the answer *How sure are you? Say that* teaches: what she didn't know, what she did, and when she would know.

"Nobody knows. In May, Mateo ran a busy morning at all three sites on a test copy, and it had room to spare. That wasn't a peak. We'll know in the second week of December."

"So we find out when Claire does," said the support engineer. Claire runs operations at Veldhaven, and the peak is hers.

In the chat, an engineer asked whether to cancel his December leave. Tyler, who runs delivery, said, "Do I change anything in the plan?" Lucía looked at the plan. Nothing she had said touched a line of it.

Her answer was sized and dated, and it sounded like a company without a plan. A minute later everyone was more worried than before, and no better prepared.

## A fact that doesn't exist yet

Ask what kind of question you have been given (*sort first*): a fact, a judgment, a commitment or a challenge. This one came as a fact, and Lucía answered it as one. But nobody could have that fact before December, and three more questions sat underneath. A judgment: which way do we go meanwhile? A commitment: what will you do, and by when? And a challenge, from the person who will be awake at four in the morning: is anyone leading this?

To those three, "nobody knows" is a shrug. You mean it as a statement about December. Left alone, it can be heard as a statement about you.

So say it, then keep going. When nobody has the answer, lead with what can be decided without it. Give the direction, the unknown at its real size, a name beside each decision, and the next test, with its day. Then say what would change your mind. None of that takes a title. It takes someone willing to be held to it.

> **Line to keep:** Nobody can promise the answer. Anyone can promise the next test.

## Paper from where?

Lucía asked for two minutes, switched off her camera and wrote five lines. Then she started again.

"That was true, and no use to you. We go through December with all three sites on the scheduler, because that's what Veldhaven bought. Nobody knows if it holds. If a site stops at night, whoever is on call decides when it goes to paper. You don't phone me first, and I'll stand behind it in the morning. Coming back is Mateo's decision. On Wednesday, if Claire agrees, we stop site two on purpose and time the switch. If it takes more than fifteen minutes, I'll ask her to run site three on paper until January. That would cost us the proof we've worked for all year. I'd still ask."

"Paper from where?" said the support engineer. "Each site prints the paper plan for two weeks after go-live. Site one stopped in October. By the middle of December, nobody will have one."

"I didn't know that," Lucía said.

"I'll ask the planners tonight what it takes to print all three," said Diego, the engineer Tolva has placed at Veldhaven.

Tyler said it back: the direction, two decisions with two names, Wednesday, fifteen minutes. "And who tells me how Wednesday went?"

"I will," said the support engineer. "By nine."

Claire's reply came that evening. "We'll print the paper plan at all three until January. Site two, Wednesday, four in the morning. My supervisor can end it with one word."

To the first answer, the support engineer had been an audience. Handed a decision, she checked whether she could carry it out, and found the hole everyone senior had missed. Then she took the test. Nobody on that call did more of the leading, and her title had nothing to do with it.

So give a decision to whoever will see the trouble first, because by the time it reaches you it is older and has lost detail. Say beforehand how far it goes, where someone else takes over, and that you'll stand behind it. Going to paper can be undone by breakfast, so it belongs to whoever answers the phone. Coming back too soon can cost a dock its morning, so it waits for Mateo.

## Where to be sure

You've been told that people follow conviction. At four in the morning that's good advice: someone deciding alone needs to know that one bad night won't turn the company round. You've also been told to admit what you don't know. That's good advice too. A team that has been promised a quiet December has no reason to rehearse the night it goes wrong.

They collide only when you say everything at one level of certainty. Be firm about the purpose, because choosing it is your job. Be exact about the part nobody can see. Be firm again about the next test, because keeping it is in your hands. And say what would change your mind before the result is in, so that changing it later is the plan at work. A plan for a month nobody can see is a bet. Call it one.

In May, Lucía wrote "on track" before she knew what the date rested on. In November she left the doubt in the open and gave it an owner, a day and a number that could prove her wrong. Choosing among paths when nobody knows the answer, and saying what would change the choice, is the strategist's habit. It's hers now.

You won't learn from this book whether December held, because its story ends first. On the evening of the 19th, nobody at Tolva knew either. Everyone knew what Wednesday was for.

## One page for a question with no answer

Lucía wrote five lines because a call was waiting. Take ten minutes, a target this book sets, and write the whole page. It's built from tools you have: sort first, the sized claim, and, for the last three lines, the preparation sheet, with December as the other side. Fill in the left; Lucía's page is on the right.

| Your page | Lucía's, 19 November |
|---|---|
| **The question**, in their words, and its kinds | "Does it hold in December?" A fact nobody has; under it, a judgment and a commitment |
| **The direction.** `We are [doing this], because [the one reason].` | Three sites on the scheduler through December, because that's what Veldhaven bought |
| **The unknown.** `Nobody knows [what]. We know [what], [basis word]. It stops at [limit].` | Whether three sites hold. Checked: one busy morning in May. Stops at a test copy, no peak |
| **Who decides.** `[name] decides [what], without asking.` | Whoever is on call sends a site to paper. Mateo decides when it comes back |
| **The next test.** `On [day], [what we do]. [Name] counts [what].` | Wednesday, site two stopped on purpose. The support engineer times the switch |
| **What would change my mind.** `If [result], then [the other path].` | Over fifteen minutes, as long as site one holds a door for a late truck: site three on paper until January |
| **If it fails anyway**, what we do, and who hears first | That site stays on paper until Mateo can say why. Claire hears it from me, that night |
| **What I won't do** | Tell anyone "it will hold" |
| **Can everyone named still keep this once it has started?** | Not without paper. Diego asks the planners |

To find the test, borrow what many teams call a pre-mortem: imagine the plan has already failed, and have each person write why. Stopping a system on purpose is old practice too, as old as the fire drill. So is handing a decision down with its reason, and asking what would stop it: crews and armies brief that way.

### Practice: the question about next year

**Situation.** A one-to-one. You lead four data engineers, and one asks: "An agent drafts most of my pipelines now. What will I be doing here in a year?"

**First draft (what a kind lead says).** "Honestly, nobody knows. It's all moving so fast. But I wouldn't worry. There will always be work for good people, and you're one of the best I have."

**What's wrong.** The unknown is true, and the comfort after it has no basis. They leave with a compliment, no direction and no date.

**Better.** "I don't know, and I won't guess. Here's what I'm choosing: on this team, a person signs every number that leaves it, however it was made. Next month, you pick one pipeline and list whatever the agent left you to correct or decide. On the 30th we read your list together. If it's empty, I was wrong about the signing, and together we choose what you learn next."

**Why it works.** The doubt is still there. Now it has a person, a day and a result that could prove the lead wrong.

**Your turn (5 minutes).** Take the question about next year that you least want to hear. Say your answer aloud in five sentences: the unknown, the direction, who decides what, the next test, what would change your mind. **Done means** a colleague who hears it once can repeat the day and the result that would change your mind.

### Practice: your page, and the name on it

**If you do only one, do this one.** Write the page for a question people near you keep asking and nobody can answer. If the test has no name, offer yours. Show it to the person beside the first decision and ask, "What would stop you from deciding this?" Change the page until you could both keep it. Then send five of its lines, from the direction to what would change your mind, to everyone who will act on them. **Done means** someone who reads only those lines can say which way you're going, what nobody knows, who decides what, and which result, on which day, would change it.

## Words to use

- "Nobody knows yet. Here's what we're doing meanwhile, and why."
- "You decide [what], without asking me first. [The other decision] stays with [name]."
- "What would stop you from deciding this?"
- "I don't know who decides. Until we hear, I suggest [step], and I'll run [the test] by [day]." (With no title.)
- "We said [result] would change the plan. It happened, so here is the new plan."

### Phrases you will hear

- "It's anyone's guess." (Nobody knows. Ask what would narrow it, and by when.)
- "We'll cross that bridge when we come to it." (We'll decide later. Ask who, and what would show it's time.)

## When it backfires

**When somebody does know.** Ask the room before you say nobody does. If you know and can't tell, say that: "I know part of this, and I can't share it before [day]." If it's only undecided and it's yours, decide.

**When they hold the power.** A client or a board may hear the unknown as a reason to take the decision from you. Give them the direction and the test first, then the unknown, and ask what they'd need to see. With no title you can't hand out decisions. You can propose the direction, ask who decides, and run the test.

**When the name is only a name.** An owner without the access, the hours or your backing has been handed the blame early. Overrule one night call the next morning, and the next one may wait for you.

**When the test can't lose.** If no result would change the plan, you've arranged a demonstration. Write the number first.

**When it becomes a habit.** Most questions have an answer. A leader who finds every one unknowable makes people wait for nothing.

**When the room expects the senior person to know.** Tell whoever must act on it first, in private, with the page in hand, and ask how they'd like the others to hear.

**In a second language.** *May*, *might* and *should* carry doubt so quietly that it can vanish. Give it a whole sentence: "Nobody has tested this." Then the day.
